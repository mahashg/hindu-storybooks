What comes next — the Diwali bedtime series

One story per night, in everyday Hindi, each ending calm — a lamp lit, a homecoming, a promise kept. Hindu festival only: the mainstream five nights plus the regional shelf, built one by one.

Night 1 — Dhanteras night

Dhanvantari rises from the ocean; Hima's son and the lamps that outwitted Yama; Kubera's prosperity thread.

Planned · Bedtime story

Night 2 — Narakasura's end

Krishna and Satyabhama free the captives; the South's dawn telling, where this morning is Diwali.

Planned · Bedtime story

Night 3 — Ayodhya's diyas

Ram, Sita and Lakshman return after fourteen years, and the city lights every doorway. The centerpiece.

Planned · Bedtime story

Night 3 — Lakshmi comes home

The ocean, the welcome, Alakshmi driven out with the cleaning, and the puja-night vrat katha.

Planned · Bedtime story

Night 4 — Govardhan

Krishna lifts the hill for seven days, and Indra's pride is washed away in the rain.

Planned · Bedtime story

Night 4 — Bali visits

The king who kept his word returns to his people, closing with the new-year customs: Bali Padyami, Bestu Varas, Chopda Pujan.

Planned · Bedtime story

Night 5 — Bhai Dooj

Yama visits Yamuna — tilak, a meal, a promise — with Chitragupta and his book of accounts as companion thread.

Planned · Bedtime story

Regional shelf — Kali Puja night (Bengal)

Bengal's Diwali: Kali dancing, Shiva beneath her feet, the night turned to her darkness-and-light.

Planned · Bedtime story

Regional shelf — Tihar's animal friends (Nepal)

Nepal's five days through the crow, the dog and the cow — Yama's messengers honoured before the lamps.

Planned · Bedtime story

Also on the shelf

The remaining Kartik festivals, Agni (Gods Before Gods, Book Two), Ganesha, Kartikeya, the Rama and Krishna arcs, and the full-ending Samudra Manthan.
